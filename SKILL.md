# UI Unslop

Run this as a final design pass. Preserve working behavior and semantic UI. Remove choices that look generated by habit rather than required by the product.

## 1. Establish intent

Inspect the rendered surface, its code, and any existing brand or design system. Identify:

- the page's job and primary action
- the intended visual voice
- semantic uses of badges, animation, and cards that must remain
- repeated structures that make the page feel templated

Do not judge a pattern in isolation. A pill can be valid status UI; a brand gradient can be intentional.

Completion criterion: each suspect choice can be classified as semantic, brand-owned, or decorative.

## 2. Audit the full surface

Read [the pattern catalog](references/pattern-catalog.md), then inspect the entire page for every named pattern. Record each occurrence as keep, revise, or remove and state why.

Prioritize high-salience repetition: the hero, section openings, repeated cards, copy, and motion system. A page-wide pattern matters more than a stray utility class.

Completion criterion: every catalog pattern has been checked, including a negative case where the existing choice is valid and should stay.

## 3. Make one coherent pass

Fix the causes, not isolated symptoms:

- Give the page one clear visual idea tied to its subject or brand.
- Use typography, hierarchy, composition, and content before decoration.
- Vary rhythm only where content changes; keep repeated content consistent.
- Write concrete copy that says what the product does.
- Keep motion scarce and purposeful.
- Reserve premium treatments for the few elements that deserve emphasis.

Prefer removal when an element carries no information. Prefer a simpler replacement when it does.

Completion criterion: every revised choice supports the page's job or visual idea, and no change breaks meaning or interaction.

## 4. Verify the result

Render the changed UI at its relevant responsive sizes and interaction states. Confirm:

- no decorative pill sits above a heading
- no unowned rainbow gradient carries a headline
- section openings do not all repeat label, heading, subtitle, and cards
- copy contains none of the catalog's banned filler phrases
- motion is limited to interactions or a small number of intentional moments
- generic icons, emoji, fake browser chrome, empty gradients, and particles do not stand in for real content
- palette, type, and surfaces follow brand hierarchy rather than tint goo or generic premium effects
- numbering, statistics, chat controls, and callouts exist only when functional and truthful
- content is not forced into uniform cards or nested frames
- one distinctive, product-specific decision is visible without harming usability
- focus, contrast, reduced-motion behavior, and core actions still work

If the project cannot be rendered, inspect the implementation and report that visual verification remains outstanding.

Completion criterion: all checks pass or each remaining exception has a semantic or brand reason.

## Output

Summarize what was removed, what replaced it, what intentionally stayed, and how the result was verified.
